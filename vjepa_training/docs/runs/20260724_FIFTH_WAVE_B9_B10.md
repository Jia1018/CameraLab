# Fifth-Wave B9/B10 Camera-Delta Direction Sweep

Date: 2026-07-24
Status: completed

B7 showed that stronger camera-delta MSE substantially increases intervention
strength, while the reconstructed feature still does not prefer the correct
crossed-camera target. B9 and B10 keep B7 fixed and add a global cosine loss
between the reconstructed and frozen V-JEPA camera-induced feature deltas:

~~~text
B9:  camera_delta_reconstruction = 8.0, camera_delta_cosine = 1.0
B10: camera_delta_reconstruction = 8.0, camera_delta_cosine = 4.0
~~~

Both remain fully self-supervised: the direction target comes from differences
inside each observed CamXTime 2x2 factor grid, not from camera pose labels.
The paired runs test whether aligning response direction improves crossed-target
preference without giving up B7's identity fidelity.

Runs started concurrently at 20:44 UTC in tmux sessions
`vjepa_fifth_wave_b9` (physical GPU 1) and `vjepa_fifth_wave_b10` (physical
GPU 0). Unit tests passed before launch (10 passed).

Both runs completed 500 steps and the deterministic 16-scene evaluation:


B9 gives a small, consistent improvement over B7: normalized response rises
12.2%, delta cosine rises 5.4%, crossed gain rises 9.4%, and target preference
becomes 0.013145 less negative. Identity error increases by 0.008291, and the
output is still closer to the original than the crossed-camera target.

B10 fails the weight-scaling hypothesis. Compared with B9, its normalized
response falls 65.6%, delta cosine falls 20.6%, crossed gain falls 60.4%, and
identity error increases by 0.026390. Late-step averages also show higher
identity reconstruction and content-invariance errors. Camera-delta cosine
weight 4 is therefore too strong and should not be pursued.
